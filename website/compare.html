Opal vs the tools that already rival Acrobat.

Plenty of free tools claim to replace Acrobat. We checked each one against its own public documentation (November 2026) and asked the same questions: What happens to your file? What does it cost? Where Opal is better — exactly

  • The only free tool here that signs with a plugged-in DSC USB token (PKCS#11) — the private key never leaves the token — and verifies signatures fully offline. Live-proven on an e-Mudhra HYP2003.
  • Zero-upload is architecture, not a promise: single local app, MIT-licensed source — read it and see that nothing phones home. Web tools process your document on their servers.
  • Built for the Indian counter: Aadhaar/passport ID photo 413×599, stamp photo, 4R/5R/6R print at 300 DPI, WhatsApp PDF under 1 MB, WhatsApp-ready photos — one click each, ~1 s per job.
  • The wheel: hold Shift/Alt/Ctrl while dragging a file — 103 actions appear around your cursor. Plus watch-folder auto-convert, right-click menu, conversion history, and a CLI (--sign/--verify-signature included).
  • Offline AI in the Full build: background removal and 2x/4x upscale run with the network unplugged.

Where Opal is not better yet — honestly

  • Brand and polish: Acrobat, Foxit and PDFgear have years of UI polish, mobile apps and support desks. Opal is a small open-source project.
  • PDF standards Acrobat Pro still owns: MDP certification, full LTV/DSS revocation embedding, preflight/PDF-X, JavaScript actions, portfolios, text reflow. On our roadmap, not in v2.2.0.
  • Distribution: published zips are Windows-only right now (macOS/Linux build from source, CI green on all three) and the exe is not code-signed yet — Windows SmartScreen will warn until we sign it.
  • No Android/iOS app (Xodo, PDFgear have them). A Flutter port is possible for conversions and .pfx signing, but USB DSC tokens do not exist on Android — the headline feature is desktop-only.

Verdict: if your job is signing PDFs with a real DSC at a counter, offline, for free — no free rival above does it. If your job is certifying PDFs to PDF/X or running JavaScript in a form, paid Acrobat Pro is still the tool. We'd rather tell you that than pretend.
