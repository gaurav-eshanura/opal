DSC signing · the Acrobat feature, free

Digitally sign PDFs — without paying Acrobat.

For years, a legally valid digital signature on a PDF meant one thing: a paid Adobe Acrobat Pro subscription. Opal does the same job free and fully offline. Plug in your DSC USB token — or drop in the .pfx / .p12 file your certificate authority gave you — and sign.

  • Real cryptographic signature computed inside the USB token — the private key never leaves the device. With a .pfx, it never leaves your computer.
  • Signs into the form's own empty signature field, exactly like Acrobat — prepared fields included. Your original is never modified; a signed copy is written.
  • Shows certificate subject, issuer, serial and validity before you commit. The PIN is asked only on a real keyboard and never stored anywhere.
  • Verify any signed PDF offline: intact or tampered, who signed, certificate chain, revoked or not.
  • Optional trusted timestamp (--tsa) from an allow-listed server — it receives only the hash of the signature, never your document.

Questions

Before you ask

Does it really work without internet?

Yes. The full build carries the cut-out model inside, so background removal works with the cable unplugged. The only things that ever touch the network are the one-time download of an optional extra model and — only if you pass --tsa while signing — an allow-listed timestamp server, which receives just the hash of the signature, never the document. Everything else — conversion, PDF editing, OCR, signing, verifying — is local.

What does it do with my files?

It reads them, changes them and writes a new file next to the original. The original is never touched, and nothing is sent anywhere. You can watch every step happen on your own screen.

Is it really a PDF editor, or a viewer with extra buttons?

It is an editor. Text, images and shapes can be added, moved and deleted. Pages can be merged, split, rotated and reordered. Forms can be built from scratch, redactions burned in, and documents protected with a password.

Which digital signature certificates work?

A .pfx / .p12 file — the format Indian certificate authorities hand out as a DSC — or the DSC USB token itself, plugged in. Opal finds the token's driver by itself, shows the certificate subject, issuer, serial and validity before you sign, and computes the signature inside the token: the private key never leaves the device. With a .pfx file the key never leaves your computer.

Do I need Adobe Acrobat to sign a PDF with my DSC?

No. That is exactly what Opal replaces. Plug in your DSC USB token or choose the .pfx / .p12 file, and Opal computes a real cryptographic signature inside the token — the key never leaves it — into the form's own empty signature field, just like Acrobat Pro. Verifying signatures is offline too: intact or tampered, signer, chain, revocation. Acrobat Pro charges a subscription for this; Opal is free.

Will it open a PDF my customer sent me?

Yes — including scanned, password-protected, upside-down and oddly-sized documents. Scans become searchable with one click.

Does it work on a slow laptop?

Everything except the AI models runs instantly. The cut-out takes a couple of seconds on a modest CPU; the lightweight U2-Net Lite model is built for exactly that case.

Is it free? Is there a paid tier?

The app is free and always will be. There is no account, no subscription and no upsell.

Is this the same product as the Mac Tangerine?

No. Opal is an independent product with its own name, mark and codebase — built for Windows, macOS and Linux, for the Indian counter and for people who need their documents to stay on their own machine.
